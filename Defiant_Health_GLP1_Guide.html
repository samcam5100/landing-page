The basics

What GLP-1 medications are

GLP-1 is a hormone your body naturally releases after you eat. It helps signal fullness and supports steady blood sugar.

GLP-1 medications work with that same signal. Used as part of a medically supervised plan, they can help reduce appetite and support portion control, which makes it easier to sustain the changes that lead to weight loss.

A few things they are not

  • They are not a quick fix. They work best alongside nutrition and activity.
  • They are not one size fits all. Your plan and dose are tailored to you.
  • They are not for everyone. A provider reviews your history first.

What the research shows

In large clinical trials, GLP-1 based medications produced average body weight reductions of roughly 15 to 22 percent over the course of about a year and a half, alongside diet and activity. These are averages from studies, not a promise of any individual result.

Nutrition

Eating well while on treatment

Because your appetite is lower, the food you do eat matters more. A few simple habits go a long way.

Prioritize protein

Including protein at each meal helps protect muscle while you lose weight.

Stay hydrated

Keep water nearby through the day. It also helps with common side effects.

Add fiber

Vegetables, fruit, and whole grains support digestion and help you feel satisfied.

Eat slowly

Smaller, slower meals are easier to tolerate. Stop when you feel comfortable.

Coaching is part of the plan

You do not have to figure this out alone. Our team offers nutrition and lifestyle guidance tailored to your routine.

What to know

Managing common side effects

Most side effects are mild and tend to ease as your body adjusts and your dose is increased slowly.

Nausea

The most common early effect. Smaller meals, eating slowly, bland foods, and staying hydrated often help, and it usually improves with time.

Digestive changes

Some people notice constipation or other changes. Fiber, fluids, and gentle movement can help.

Start low, go slow

Your provider begins at a low dose and increases gradually. This is the biggest reason side effects tend to stay manageable.

When to reach out

Contact your provider if side effects are severe or do not improve. Seek care right away for severe abdominal pain or signs of an allergic reaction.

Be prepared

Questions to ask your provider

A good consultation is a conversation. Here are questions worth bringing.

  • Based on my health history, is a GLP-1 program a reasonable option for me?
  • What medication and dose would you recommend, and why?
  • What side effects should I watch for, and how do we manage them?
  • How will we track my progress, and how often will we check in?
  • What does the full cost look like, and what is included?
  • What happens if I want to pause, switch, or stop?

Bring your full history

Tell your provider about all medications and conditions. It helps them keep your care safe and tailored to you.
